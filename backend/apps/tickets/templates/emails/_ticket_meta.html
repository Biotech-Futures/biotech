{% comment %} Shared block: the ticket's identity, where it stands, and the way back to it. Every ticket email carries it so the requester never has to open the portal just to find out which enquiry an email is about. The button is the same "bulletproof" markup as login.html and password_reset.html — a VML rectangle for Outlook, an anchor for everything else. It replaced a line of 12px grey text at the foot of the email, which was the only route back to the conversation and read as small print. The recipients are school students and their guardians; the way to answer must not be the least visible thing on the page. Two of the strings below can arrive as one unbreakable word, and a table is never narrower than the longest word it holds. The subject is typed by the requester, and "I cannot open " is the most natural way for a student to report a broken page. The address under the button is one word by definition. Without a break rule either of them widens the whole card past the screen, and on a 320px phone the button's own label ends up off to the right of it. The subject uses overflow-wrap so ordinary subjects still break at spaces; the address uses word-break, because a URL may be split anywhere. The greeting at the top of each of the three ticket emails is the third such string and carries the same overflow-wrap rule. It is the account holder's own first name, which is a 255 character field the account holder can edit. Named here because this is where the rule is explained; the test that keeps all three honest does not work from a list of places, it fills every field a requester can type into and looks for wherever the words come out.
